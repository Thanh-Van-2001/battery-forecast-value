\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether a more accurate load forecast lowers a building's bill when it is used to schedule a battery, in a pre-specified study of \res{nKept} buildings. Under a demand charge, the ranking of five standard forecasters by MAE did not agree with their ranking by saving. The sign of the error explains why: under-forecasts at charging hours set new monthly peaks while over-forecasts only keep charge in reserve, so the least accurate, peak-conservative forecaster saved the most, and an asymmetric score agreed with value where the MAE did not. How the schedule was executed mattered more than the forecaster, since open-loop schedules raised the bill in most buildings while a simple real-time guard made them save. Without a demand charge, accuracy did not matter at all. Forecasts for storage should be judged by asymmetric, decision-relevant scores under the controller that will use them.
